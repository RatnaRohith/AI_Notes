# KNOWLEDGE IN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

## 1. Definition of Knowledge

In Artificial Intelligence (AI), knowledge is the collection of facts, concepts, relationships, rules, and information about a particular domain that an AI system uses to understand situations, reason, solve problems, make decisions, and take actions.

Knowledge = Information + Relationships + Rules + Ability to Reason

Example:

1. Ravi is a student.
2. Every student has a student ID.
3. Ravi is a student.

Conclusion: Ravi has a student ID.

The AI system uses existing knowledge and rules to derive new knowledge.

--------------------------------------------------

## 2. Data vs Information vs Knowledge

DATA:
Data consists of raw facts, values, or observations.

Example:
80

INFORMATION:
Information is processed data that has meaning.

Example:
Ravi scored 80 marks in an examination.

KNOWLEDGE:
Knowledge combines information with rules and relationships to support reasoning.

Example:
Rule: If a student's marks are greater than or equal to 40, the student passes.

Fact: Ravi scored 80 marks.

Conclusion: Ravi passed the examination.

The relationship can be represented as:

Data
  ↓
Information
  ↓
Knowledge
  ↓
Reasoning
  ↓
Decision or Action

--------------------------------------------------

## 3. Components of Knowledge

Knowledge in AI can contain the following components.

### 3.1 Facts

Facts are statements considered true within a particular knowledge base.

Examples:
- Ravi is a student.
- The Earth revolves around the Sun.
- Water freezes at approximately 0°C under standard atmospheric pressure.

### 3.2 Objects

Objects are entities that exist in a particular domain.

Examples:
- Ravi
- Car
- College
- Book

### 3.3 Properties

Properties describe the characteristics of objects.

Examples:
- Car → Red
- Book → 500 pages
- Student → Roll number 101

### 3.4 Relationships

Relationships describe connections between objects.

Examples:
- Ravi → studies_at → ABC College
- Ravi → owns → Car
- ABC College → located_in → Andhra Pradesh

### 3.5 Rules

Rules describe conditions and conclusions that can be used for reasoning.

Example:

IF student attendance is greater than or equal to 75%
THEN student is eligible for the examination.

### 3.6 Constraints

Constraints specify restrictions or conditions that must be satisfied.

Example:

A student cannot attend two different classroom sessions at the same time.

--------------------------------------------------

## 4. Types of Knowledge in AI

### 4.1 Factual Knowledge

Factual knowledge describes what is known to be true.

Examples:
- Delhi is the capital of India.
- Ravi is a student.
- The Sun is a star.

### 4.2 Relational Knowledge

Relational knowledge describes relationships between entities.

Example:

Ravi → studies_at → ABC College

ABC College → located_in → Andhra Pradesh

### 4.3 Procedural Knowledge

Procedural knowledge describes how to perform a task or activity.

Example: Starting a car

1. Insert the key.
2. Turn the key or activate the start system.
3. Start the engine.
4. Release the control when the engine starts.

### 4.4 Heuristic Knowledge

Heuristic knowledge is based on experience, practical judgment, or rules of thumb.

Example:

IF the road is wet
THEN reduce driving speed and maintain a safe distance.

A heuristic is useful but does not guarantee a correct result in every situation.

### 4.5 Common-Sense Knowledge

Common-sense knowledge represents everyday facts and expectations that people generally understand.

Examples:
- A glass dropped onto a hard floor may break.
- People generally need food and water.
- Unsupported objects may fall because of gravity.

### 4.6 Meta-Knowledge

Meta-knowledge is knowledge about other knowledge, including its source, reliability, or applicability.

Example:

Rule A is based on verified information, whereas Rule B is based on an uncertain observation.

An AI system may use this information to determine which rule is more reliable.

--------------------------------------------------

## 5. Knowledge Base

A Knowledge Base (KB) is an organized collection of facts, rules, relationships, and other knowledge used by an AI system.

A knowledge base may contain:

- Facts
- Rules
- Objects
- Properties
- Relationships
- Constraints

Example: Medical Knowledge Base

Fact 1: The patient has a fever.

Fact 2: The patient has a cough.

Rule:

IF the patient has a fever AND a cough
THEN consider the possibility of an infection.

The rule suggests a possibility, not a confirmed diagnosis.

A knowledge base stores knowledge, while an inference engine applies reasoning methods to derive conclusions from that knowledge.

--------------------------------------------------

## 6. How AI Uses Knowledge

Knowledge supports several important AI activities.

### 6.1 Reasoning

Reasoning is the process of deriving conclusions from existing facts and rules.

Example:

Fact: Socrates is a human.

Rule: All humans are mortal.

Conclusion: Socrates is mortal.

Logical representation:

Human(Socrates)

∀x (Human(x) → Mortal(x))

Therefore:

Mortal(Socrates)

### 6.2 Problem Solving

AI uses knowledge to identify possible solutions to a problem.

General process:

Current State
     ↓
Identify Available Actions
     ↓
Evaluate Possible Outcomes
     ↓
Select a Solution
     ↓
Reach the Goal

Example:

A navigation system uses information about roads, distances, and traffic conditions to find a suitable route.

### 6.3 Decision Making

Knowledge helps an AI system choose an appropriate action.

Example:

IF traffic_light = RED
THEN STOP

IF traffic_light = GREEN AND the road is clear
THEN PROCEED when safe.

### 6.4 Planning

Planning involves identifying a sequence of actions required to achieve a goal.

Example:

Goal: Reach College.

1. Leave home.
2. Walk to the bus stop.
3. Board the appropriate bus.
4. Get down at the college stop.
5. Reach the college.

### 6.5 Prediction

AI can use existing knowledge and observed patterns to predict possible outcomes.

Example:

Observation: Dark clouds are present.

Additional information: Weather data indicates a high probability of rainfall.

Prediction: Rain is likely.

A prediction is not always certain.

--------------------------------------------------

## 7. Knowledge Representation

Knowledge Representation (KR) is the process of encoding knowledge into a structured form that a computer can store, process, and use for reasoning.

Natural language:

All humans are mortal.

Logical representation:

∀x (Human(x) → Mortal(x))

Given:

Human(Ravi)

The system can infer:

Mortal(Ravi)

Common knowledge representation techniques include:

1. Logic
2. Production Rules
3. Semantic Networks
4. Frames
5. Ontologies
6. Probabilistic Representations

Each technique is suitable for different types of knowledge and reasoning tasks.

--------------------------------------------------

## 8. Knowledge-Based AI Architecture

A basic knowledge-based AI system consists of the following components.

1. Environment:
   The real-world surroundings in which the system operates.

2. Input or Perception:
   Information obtained from users, sensors, or other sources.

3. Knowledge Base:
   Stores facts, rules, and relationships.

4. Inference Engine:
   Applies reasoning methods to the available knowledge.

5. Decision:
   Selects an appropriate response or action.

6. Action:
   Executes the selected response.

Architecture:

Environment
     ↓
Input / Perception
     ↓
Knowledge Base
     ↕
Inference Engine
     ↓
Decision
     ↓
Action

Example: Obstacle-Avoiding Robot

Knowledge:
- An obstacle is detected ahead.
- The robot should avoid obstacles.

Reasoning:
- An obstacle is present in the robot's path.
- Continuing forward may cause a collision.

Decision:
- Change direction or stop.

Action:
- The robot stops or moves along a safe path.

--------------------------------------------------

## 9. Knowledge in Different AI Applications

### 9.1 Expert Systems

Expert systems use domain-specific facts and rules to provide recommendations or solve specialized problems.

Example:
A medical expert system may use symptoms and medical rules to suggest possible conditions for further examination.

### 9.2 Robotics

Robots use knowledge about objects, locations, obstacles, actions, and goals.

Example:
A robot uses its map and obstacle information to navigate a room.

### 9.3 Game-Playing AI

Game-playing AI uses game rules, possible moves, board states, and strategies to select actions.

Example:
A chess program evaluates possible moves to select a promising move.

### 9.4 Natural Language Processing

Natural Language Processing (NLP) systems use language patterns, context, and semantic relationships to interpret text or speech.

Example:
Understanding the meaning of a word based on the sentence in which it appears.

### 9.5 Recommendation Systems

Recommendation systems use information about users, products, preferences, and previous interactions to suggest relevant items.

Example:
A movie recommendation system suggests science-fiction movies to a user who enjoys that genre.

--------------------------------------------------

## 10. Knowledge vs Intelligence

Knowledge alone does not guarantee intelligent behavior.

Example:

Knowledge:
A red traffic light means STOP.

For an AI-controlled vehicle:

1. Perception: Detect the traffic light.
2. Recognition: Identify that the light is red.
3. Knowledge: Apply the rule that a red light requires stopping.
4. Reasoning: Determine the appropriate response.
5. Action: Apply the brakes safely.

Therefore, intelligent behavior can involve a combination of:

- Knowledge
- Perception
- Reasoning
- Learning
- Planning
- Decision Making
- Action

--------------------------------------------------

## 11. Importance of Knowledge in AI

Knowledge is important because it enables an AI system to:

1. Understand information about its environment.
2. Represent facts and relationships.
3. Infer new conclusions.
4. Solve problems.
5. Make decisions.
6. Plan a sequence of actions.
7. Predict possible outcomes.
8. Apply domain-specific expertise.
9. Explain certain conclusions when the system supports explanations.
10. Use information to achieve defined goals.

--------------------------------------------------

## 12. Summary

Knowledge in Artificial Intelligence consists of facts, concepts, relationships, rules, and other information that an AI system uses to reason and perform tasks.

A Knowledge Base stores organized knowledge.

An Inference Engine applies reasoning methods to the knowledge.

Knowledge Representation provides a structured way to express knowledge so that a computer can process it.

The basic process is:

Knowledge
    ↓
Knowledge Representation
    ↓
Reasoning
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Action

FINAL DEFINITION:

Knowledge in Artificial Intelligence is a collection of facts, concepts, relationships, rules, and experiences about a particular domain that can be represented in a machine-processable form and used by an AI system for reasoning, problem solving, decision making, planning, and action.
