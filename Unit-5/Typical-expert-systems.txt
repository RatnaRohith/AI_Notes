Typical Expert Systems

An Expert System is an AI program that imitates the decision-making ability of a human expert in a specific domain. It uses a knowledge base and an inference engine to solve problems and provide recommendations or diagnoses.

1. MYCIN

MYCIN was one of the most famous early expert systems, developed at Stanford University in the 1970s.

Purpose

MYCIN was designed to:

Diagnose bacterial infections
Recommend appropriate antibiotics
Determine suitable dosage based on patient information
How it worked

MYCIN used:

Production rules
Backward chaining
Certainty factors to handle uncertainty

Example rule:

IF
    the patient has a bacterial infection
    AND the infection is meningitis
THEN
    recommend an appropriate antibiotic.
Importance

MYCIN demonstrated that an expert system could perform complex medical reasoning at a level comparable to specialists for a limited diagnostic domain.

2. DART

DART stands for Dynamic Analysis and Replanning Tool.

It was developed for the U.S. military to assist with logistics and transportation planning.

Purpose

DART was used to:

Plan transportation and logistics
Allocate resources
Schedule movements
Deal with changing situations
Re-plan when circumstances changed
Example

Suppose military equipment has to be transported from several locations.

DART can reason about:

Available vehicles
        ↓
Available routes
        ↓
Equipment requirements
        ↓
Transportation schedule
        ↓
Optimal logistics plan
Importance

DART demonstrated the usefulness of AI for large-scale planning, scheduling, and logistics.

3. XCON

XCON was an expert system developed by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC).

It was also known as R1.

Purpose

XCON was designed to help configure computer systems according to customer requirements.

For example, a customer might order:

Computer system
    ├── Processor
    ├── Memory
    ├── Storage
    ├── Controllers
    └── Peripherals

XCON determined:

Which components were required
Which components were compatible
How components should be configured
What additional components were needed
Importance

XCON became one of the earliest major commercially successful expert systems.

It showed that expert systems could provide significant benefits in an industrial environment.

4. Comparison of Typical Expert Systems
Expert System	Domain	Main Purpose	Important Technique
MYCIN	Medicine	Diagnose infections and recommend antibiotics	Rules, backward chaining, certainty factors

XCON	Computer manufacturing	Configure computer systems	Rule-based reasoning
Key Point for Examination

The three systems illustrate different applications of expert systems:

MYCIN → Medical Diagnosis
DART → Logistics and Planning
XCON → Computer Configuration
